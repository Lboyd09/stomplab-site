The slow part

Tone-chasing a song in HX Edit eats the night.

You already own the Stomp. You know the riff. What you do not know is whether they used a Dual Rectifier or a JCM, whether the chorus lives in the verse, or which HX block is actually the Small Clone. CustomTone is a haystack. You swap amps for an hour and still miss the snapshot that makes the song work.

  • Research is the job Recorded tones are a chain, not a single “high gain” amp. Miss one pedal and it is a different song.
  • HX Edit is a workshop, not a librarian Blocks, DSP, snapshots, and footswitch mode are easy to get almost right — and still wrong on the floor.
  • A preset without sections is a still photo Verse quiet, chorus slammed, solo with the modulation back on. That mapping is the patch.

Three real steps

Open a song. Download .hlx. Import, then Snapshot mode.

No account. Auth is off. Featured songs are the conversion path.

  1. Pick a featured song

    Open Smells Like Teen Spirit, Enter Sandman, or another featured rig in the lab. No API key. To research any other track, paste a free Gemini key in Settings — it stays in your browser, not on this marketing site.

  2. Download the .hlx

    StompLab maps the original amps and pedals onto HX models, stays inside the Stomp’s block budget, and writes a preset file you can actually load. Repeats of featured rigs are free for everyone.

  3. HX Edit → File → Import

    USB the unit, import the file, then put the Stomp in Snapshot mode. FS1–FS3 recall verse / chorus / solo (or intro / rhythm / lead, depending on the song). Leave it in Stomp mode and the switches will not do that.

HX Stomp XL

Same .hlx family, same firmware target. More switches on the chassis; the song still wants Snapshot mode for the section changes.

Supported Firmware 3.80 HX Edit import

Not claimed: Helix Floor, Helix LT, Helix Rack, or Helix Stadium. Those units are a different layout and DSP map. StompLab is built for the Stomp and the Stomp XL only.
